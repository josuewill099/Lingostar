If you're reading this, you've probably already decided to leave Duolingo. The question is what to use instead — and the answer depends entirely on what's actually frustrating you about Duolingo in the first place.

Most "best Duolingo alternatives" articles list 10 apps and pretend they're interchangeable. They're not. The right replacement for someone who hates the gamification is completely different from the right replacement for someone who's plateaued at intermediate. So before the list, the diagnosis.

I speak five languages (Spanish, English, German, Dutch, Polish — Spanish is my native, the others are learned), I've used every major language app for years, and I write about SEO in regulated industries by day. Below is what I'd actually recommend, ranked by what's broken in your relationship with the green owl.

## TL;DR

*   **Frustrated with the AI features and layoffs?** → Babbel or Busuu (both have humans involved).
*   **Plateaued at intermediate?** → italki tutor or LingQ. Duolingo can't take you past A2.
*   **Want to actually speak the language?** → italki or Speak. Duolingo doesn't teach speaking.
*   **Hate the gamification and streaks?** → Pimsleur (audio, no streaks) or Lingoda (live classes).
*   **Want free?** → ChatGPT + Memrise free tier + Anki. Better than Duolingo Super, costs nothing.

## Why People Are Leaving Duolingo in 2026

Three things happened to Duolingo over the last 18 months that pushed a lot of long-term users out.

The company announced an "AI-first" strategy and laid off contract translators, replacing much of the content production with AI. Some users felt this was a values shift; many noticed the lessons getting weirder, with example sentences that read like AI hallucinations.

The "Diamond league" gamification escalated to the point where Reddit threads about quitting Duolingo became a regular occurrence. And the actual learning outcome — the massive gap between _"I'm 1,200 days into Duolingo"_ and _"I can have a conversation"_ — became impossible to ignore once people started comparing notes.

If any of those resonate, you're not alone. The good news: there are better tools for almost every specific frustration.

## What's Actually Broken About Duolingo (And Which Alternative Fixes It)

Different problems need different replacements. Pick the diagnosis that matches yours.

### Problem 1: "I've been on Duolingo for years and still can't speak"

This is the most common complaint, and it's not your fault. Duolingo doesn't really teach speaking. The "speaking exercises" use weak speech recognition that marks you correct for almost anything you say. You're recognizing the language, not producing it.

*   **The Fix:** **italki**. Book three lessons with a real tutor from your target country — about $30 total. You'll progress more in those three hours than in three months of Duolingo. If italki isn't your style, **Speak** gives you AI conversation practice with real pronunciation feedback for $20/month. Either of those will fix the speaking gap fast.

### Problem 2: "I'm tired of the gamification and streak guilt"

Duolingo's growth came from gamification. The cost is that learning starts to feel like compulsive behavior — you're not opening it because you want to learn, you're opening it because the streak is at 847 days and you can't bring yourself to kill it.

*   **The Fix:** **Pimsleur** for audio-only learning with zero gamification — just 30-minute lessons you do when you have time. Or **Lingoda** for scheduled live classes (the calendar schedule replaces the streak as your accountability mechanism). Or just an **italki** tutor with a recurring weekly slot.

### Problem 3: "I've plateaued — Duolingo isn't taking me past intermediate"

Duolingo tops out at roughly A2 (basic conversational) for most languages. It rarely gets you to B1 (independent user), and never to B2+. Many users reach this plateau around year two.

*   **The Fix:** Stop using a beginner app. Switch to **LingQ** (read native content with built-in vocabulary tracking — best for upper-intermediate readers), an **italki** tutor for advanced conversation, or **Anki** with curated decks for systematic vocabulary expansion. None of these are beginner-friendly. All of them push you forward.

### Problem 4: "The recent AI changes feel off"

If the AI-first pivot bothered you on principle — or if you've noticed the sentences getting weirder — you want apps that still keep humans in the loop.

*   **The Fix:** **Babbel** (lessons written by real linguists, separate Latin American and European Spanish tracks honestly maintained). **Busuu** (the standout feature is the community — real native speakers correct your written exercises, which is increasingly rare). **Lingoda** (live classes with certified teachers).

### Problem 5: "I want free, but Duolingo's ads are unbearable"

Free Duolingo is fine. Free Duolingo with the modern ad load is not.

*   **The Fix:** Build a free stack that beats Super Duolingo at $7/month. Use **Memrise's free tier** for vocabulary through real native-speaker video clips, **Anki** with a community deck for systematic retention, **ChatGPT** for daily conversation practice, and **Forvo** + **YouGlish** for pronunciation. Total cost: zero. Better outcomes than Super Duolingo.

### Problem 6: "I want a real curriculum, not random lessons"

Duolingo's curriculum is opaque. You're moved through "units" and "sections," but it's hard to know where you actually are in standard CEFR terms.

*   **The Fix:** **Babbel** is the cleanest curriculum on the market — you progress through clearly labeled levels with clear competencies. **Lingoda** is even more rigorous if you want CEFR-aligned coursework. Both feel like real adult education rather than a playground.

## My Top 5 Duolingo Replacements (With Reasoning)

### 1\. Babbel — Best Overall Replacement

If you want something that feels like Duolingo but actually works, Babbel is it. Same lesson-based format, similar 10–15 minute sessions, but with curriculum written by linguists, example sentences that sound like real human speech, and grammar explanations that don't insult your intelligence.

The biggest qualitative difference: Babbel tells you _why_ something is grammatically correct, where Duolingo just marks you wrong and moves on. After two years of Duolingo, you don't know why _"le"_ is sometimes _"él"_ and sometimes not. After two months of Babbel, you do.

*   **Cost:** ~$14/month on annual (frequently discounted to $7–8).
*   **Best for:** Complete beginners to upper-intermediate. Multiple languages.
*   **Skip if:** You're already past B1 (you'll outgrow it).

### 2\. italki — Best for Actually Getting Fluent

The single highest-leverage move you can make is replacing app time with real conversation time. italki is the marketplace where you do that. Browse tutors, filter by country and price, and book individual sessions starting around $8/hour for community tutors.

What makes italki the right Duolingo replacement specifically: it forces you to produce language under pressure, in real time, with a human who'll correct you. That's the exact thing Duolingo doesn't do. Three weekly hour-long sessions over a month will move you further than a year of automated text-tapping.

*   **Cost:** $8–25 per lesson depending on tutor.
*   **Best for:** Anyone past complete beginner. 150+ languages.
*   **Skip if:** You absolutely cannot commit to scheduled sessions.

### 3\. Speak — Best AI Alternative

If part of what you liked about Duolingo was that it was always available and didn't require scheduling, but you also want to actually learn to speak, Speak is the replacement. AI conversation practice with real speech recognition that gives you syllable-level pronunciation feedback. It is closer to a real tutor than any other AI app.

*   **Cost:** ~$20/month.
*   **Best for:** Intermediate learners who freeze when speaking.
*   **Skip if:** You're an absolute beginner with no foundation yet.

### 4\. Busuu — Best Community-Driven Alternative

Busuu does one thing none of the big apps do: real native speakers from the community correct your written exercises. You write a paragraph, post it, and someone in your target country fixes it for you (and you fix theirs in return). It feels deeply human in an industry going the opposite direction.

The curriculum itself is fine — not quite as polished as Babbel, but solid. The free tier is genuinely useful (a rarity in this category).

*   **Cost:** Free tier available; ~$14/month for premium features.
*   **Best for:** Writers, people who care about community context, and free users.
*   **Skip if:** You primarily want real-time spoken conversation practice.

### 5\. Pimsleur — Best for the Gamification-Fatigued

If what you really hated about Duolingo was the constant nudging, the streaks, the leagues, the notifications, the feeling of being managed by an algorithm — Pimsleur is the antidote. Audio-only. 30-minute lessons. No gamification. No screens. Same proven method since the 1960s.

You listen, you respond out loud, you progress through structured repetition. It's calm. It works.

*   **Cost:** ~$20/month.
*   **Best for:** Commuters, audio learners, anyone tired of screen-based gamification.
*   **Skip if:** You need immediate visual reinforcement or text to learn.

## Honorable Mentions

*   **Memrise:** Excellent supplement focused on vocabulary through real native-speaker video clips. Free tier is very useful.
*   **LingQ:** Reading-based learning for intermediate and above. You import any text — articles, books, podcast transcripts — and the app tracks your vocabulary recognition over time.
*   **Anki:** Free, open-source spaced repetition flashcards. Hardcore. Has a learning curve, but will outperform any paid vocabulary app once you set it up.
*   **Lingoda:** Live group or 1-on-1 classes with certified teachers, structured around CEFR levels. The "Sprint" program is the best financial accountability mechanism in the industry.
*   **Mango Languages:** Often free through public libraries. Decent curriculum, multiple dialects honestly distinguished.
*   **Rosetta Stone:** Skip it. It hasn't aged well and the price-to-value ratio is poor compared to modern alternatives.
*   **Mondly:** Skip it. It suffers from the exact same surface-level gamification problems as Duolingo.

## How to Actually Quit Duolingo

The biggest reason people don't switch isn't lack of better options. **It's the streak.**

The streak is a classic sunk cost. If you've been on Duolingo for 800 days, those 800 days don't get longer because you keep going — and they don't get shorter when you quit. The question is what the next 800 days look like, and whether you want them to be more of the same or actually lead you to fluency.

Here's the practical move: download whatever app(s) you've decided on from this article. Use them for one week alongside Duolingo. After that week, intentionally let your streak die. You'll feel a weird pang for about 48 hours, and then you'll feel completely free.

## FAQ

### What's the best Duolingo alternative for Spanish?

Babbel for structure or italki for fastest progress. The combined stack of Babbel + italki + Speak outperforms Duolingo for most learners by a wide margin.

### What's the best free Duolingo alternative?

Memrise free tier + ChatGPT + Anki + Forvo. It costs $0 and outperforms Duolingo Super. If you want one single free app, Memrise is the closest direct replacement.

### Is Babbel actually better than Duolingo?

For learning to use the language, yes — clearly. Babbel's curriculum is written by linguists, example sentences sound like real speech, and grammar explanations are usable. Duolingo wins only on price, gamification, and habit-forming UX.

### Why do so many people quit Duolingo?

Three reasons: realization that years of streaks don't equal fluency; the AI-first strategy and recent layoffs alienated some users on principle; and ad load and gamification fatigue compound over time until the app feels like an obligation.

### Can I learn a language without Duolingo at all?

Yes — most fluent adult learners didn't use Duolingo. The traditional path is a structured course (Babbel, Lingoda, Pimsleur, or a textbook), plus regular speaking practice (italki tutor), plus consumption of native content (TV, podcasts, books).

### Which Duolingo alternative is best for intermediate learners?

italki for speaking practice, LingQ for reading-based progression, and Anki for vocabulary expansion. Duolingo specifically struggles past A2, which is exactly where most users plateau.

### Is Duolingo Super worth it?

For most users, no. The Super features (no ads, mistakes review, unlimited hearts) offer convenience, not learning improvement. If you have $7/month to spend on language learning, almost any other option in this article will give you more value.
